# Job search in Auckland - starter tips

This shared starter file is loaded the first time the Knowledge Base starts. The admin can delete it
or upload better documents. Each user can also upload their own private documents (CVs, job adverts,
company notes, interview notes). Check facts that change often (pay rates, visa rules) on official websites.

## Where to look for jobs

Common job websites in New Zealand are SEEK (seek.co.nz), Trade Me Jobs (trademe.co.nz/a/jobs),
Indeed (nz.indeed.com), LinkedIn Jobs and Student Job Search (sjs.co.nz) for students.
Many employers also post jobs on their own careers page. Recruitment agencies can help with casual
warehouse, cleaning and hospitality work.

## A New Zealand style CV

A New Zealand CV is usually short: one or two pages. Put contact details at the top: name, phone,
email and suburb or city. A full home address, photo, date of birth and marital status are not needed.

Order: a short personal statement (two or three lines), key skills, work experience (newest first),
education, and "references available on request" or the names of referees.

Write what you did with simple action words: cleaned, prepared, served, packed, helped, organised.
Use the same words as the job advert when they are true for you. This helps the applicant
tracking system (ATS) and the person reading it.

Make a different CV for each type of job. For a kitchen hand job, show kitchen, cleaning, food safety
and teamwork experience first. For an IT job, show IT experience, projects and qualifications.

Never invent employers, job titles, dates, qualifications or references. An honest CV that shows
real transferable skills is better than a long CV.

## Cover letters and application emails

Keep a cover letter to three or four short paragraphs: which job you are applying for, why you fit
(two or three real examples), your availability, and a thank you. An application email can be even
shorter and should have the CV attached as PDF. Use a clear subject line, for example
"Application - Kitchen Hand - Your Name".

## Availability and work rights

Say clearly when you can work, for example "Monday to Friday any time, weekends after 3 pm".
Employers in hospitality, cleaning and retail often need evenings and weekends.

If you are on a visa, answer work-rights questions truthfully. Student visas often limit the number of
work hours during study. Check your own visa conditions on the Immigration New Zealand website
(immigration.govt.nz) before you promise hours to an employer.

## Interview tips

Before the interview: read the job advert again, look at the company website, and plan how to get there.
Arrive 10 minutes early. Bring a copy of your CV.

Common questions: Tell me about yourself. Why do you want this job? What experience do you have?
When can you work? How do you handle a busy shift? Tell me about a time you worked in a team.
Do you have any questions for us?

Answer with short real examples: what was the situation, what did you do, and what was the result.

Good questions to ask the employer: What does a normal shift look like? What training do you give?
Who will I work with? When will I hear back?

## When the employer thinks you are overqualified

Explain honestly why you want this job now, for example: you are studying and want steady part-time
work, you enjoy practical team work, and you will be reliable and on time. Do not hide your study,
but focus on the skills that matter for this job.

## Follow up

If you have not heard back after about one week, send a short polite email or call to ask about your
application. Keep a record of every job you applied for, the date, and the follow-up date.
